# Roads survey (from 11 Oct 2026)

Avi's brief: survey every street against aerial imagery, Street View and open data, and rebuild each one exactly: lanes and widths, markings, bike and bus lanes, crosswalks, stop lines, curb ramps, medians, plazas, signals on their real poles, hydrants, manholes, Citi Bike docks, bus stops and parking signs, with sources and Street View dates per block.

## Done

- **(a) Painted medians** (11 Oct). 148 of the 231 planimetric medians are subtype 360010, painted: white hatching on the asphalt. They had been extruded as raised paving slabs, which put strips of sidewalk down Fifth Avenue and other roads. They are now flush hatching (outline plus 45 degree hatch lines 2.4 m apart); curb medians (34) and traffic islands (49) stay raised. Every other slab that overlaps a centreline was checked on orthoimagery: those are centreline offsets, not artifacts.
- **(c) Lane markings** (13 Oct), generated by `tools/build-markings.mjs` into `dist/campus/data/street-markings.js`. For each block the cross-section is measured by casting rays from the centreline to the curb every 4 m; painted buffers near a curb mark a bike lane outside them; parking lanes (2.4 m) go against the carriageway edges, then a conventional or curbside bike lane (1.5 m) on the side OSM gives, then a bus lane (3.3 m, red) where NYC DOT has one, and the travel lanes share the rest. Double yellow between opposing traffic, white dashes 3 m on, 9 m off (NYC's 10 ft and 30 ft) between lanes going the same way, solid white along bike and bus lanes, and 60 cm stop lines across the approach lanes at every intersection with a signal or stop sign. All lines stop short of the intersection.
- **Green paint** (13 Oct): every one of the 121 bike-lane stretches was looked at on the orthoimagery; 25 are green (`green-paint.md`), the rest are plain asphalt or could not be read under building shadow.

- **(d) Crosswalks and signals** (13 Oct). Crosswalks follow each OSM crossing's marking: bars for `zebra` (the city's standard high-visibility pattern: 0.6 m bars on 1.2 m centres, 3 m long), bars between edge lines for `ladder`, two lines for `lines`, nothing where OSM says unmarked. Signals: at every intersection with an OSM traffic signal within 22 m (753 corner poles), a grey pole 0.6 m in from the curb on each corner, carrying a yellow three-lamp vehicle head for each street (facing traffic coming through the intersection) and a black pedestrian head (hand and walking figure). Appearance checked on Street View at Broadway and E 8th St (Apr 2026): yellow heads on grey poles. They run a 90 s cycle per intersection (42 s green, 3 s amber, walk 30 s then flashing hand) with a fixed offset each; dead in 2126. Not yet: signals on the same pole as the street light where they are shared, mast arms on the wide avenues, and LPI or Barnes dance timing.

- **(e) Street furniture** (13 Oct), generated by `tools/build-furniture.mjs` into `dist/campus/data/street-furniture.js` and drawn by `dist/campus/furniture.js`: 1,461 curb ramps (NYC DOT ramp points; red detectable warning pads, the NYC Street Design Manual's colour on concrete, 0.6 m deep and as wide as the recorded ramp), 859 fire hydrants (black body, silver bonnet, as on Street View at University Pl and E 9th St, Sep 2024), 2,791 parking sign poles carrying the DOT's 5,431 signs (signs within 0.6 m share a pole; white plates facing the roadway), 91 bus stop poles (OSM), and 54 Citi Bike stations with every dock (GBFS capacity; bikes in about two docks of three; 38 stations sit in the parking lane, the rest on the sidewalk; empty in 2126). Each item is turned to its nearest curb. Pedestrian signal heads are yellow, like the vehicle heads (Street View, same corner). Not yet: the ramps' sloped curb cuts themselves, sign lettering (the plates are blank), bus stop route plates, manholes (no open data).

- **(f) Street View block checks, first pass** (13 Oct): the four sides of the park, Fifth Av, University Pl, LaGuardia Pl and W 4th St (captures Apr and May 2026, Washington Sq W Jul 2022). Corrections live in `blocks.json` (keyed by centreline id, with the capture month): the park sides have parking only on the building side (the centreline data says both), bike lanes along the park curb on all four sides, and solid white parking lines where they are painted. Bike lanes that have not been checked for green paint now get none.

## Known limits

- The orthoimagery and the map disagree by 2 to 5 m in places (building lean and registration), so it is used to decide what exists, not to place it.
- Where OSM has no cycleway side for a one-way street, a conventional lane goes on the right. These are the first to check on Street View.
- Lane counts and widths come from the city's centreline attributes; where the measured carriageway is too narrow for them, the lane count is reduced so no lane is under 2.7 m.

## Pole audit (14 Oct 2026)

Avi found sign and light poles standing in the lanes of W 8th St. The cause: the NYC DOT parking-sign points are not where the posts stand; most lie along the street centre line at the sign's distance from the intersection, and 2,076 of the 2,791 sign posts built from them stood in the roadway. Now (tools/lib/curbs.mjs, shared by the build tools and tests/poles.test.js):
- Parking signs are moved to the curb on their own side of the street (`side_of_street`), 0.45 m in from the curb face. The `support` column decides the post: DR, POLE, PS, SPIG, VAB and the rarer codes get a post; SAME shares one; LP (lamp post), TS (signal pole) and BUSPOLE hang on poles already modelled, so they make no new post (880 signs). Signs within 0.8 m share a post. 177 signs with no curb on their side within 25 m are left out. Result: 2,124 posts, none in the roadway.
- Hydrants in the roadway (45, offsets in the DEP points) move to the curb, 0.6 m in; one with no curb near is left out.
- Street lamps in the roadway in OSM (16) move to the curb, 0.5 m in.
- Signal poles whose corner bisector met a gap in the curbs (4) move to the curb.
- tests/poles.test.js fails if any sign post, hydrant, bus stop pole, signal pole or street lamp stands in the roadway (roadbed minus sidewalks, plazas and raised medians).
- Found along the way: about 100 street trees in the core "in the roadway" are on real sidewalks whose planimetric sidewalk polygon is missing (e.g. the north side of the street at x -150 to -60, n 280 to 310). Those are roadway-surface gaps for the artifact sweep, not misplaced trees.
- Before and after: RESEARCH/screenshots/w8-poles-before-after-2026-10-14.png (W 8th St from Fifth Avenue looking west, noon).

## Bike lanes as New York paints them (14 Oct 2026)

Every bike lane piece built from the NYC DOT bike routes (128 pieces) was checked by eye on NYS orthoimagery: the block's entry end (with the intersection behind it) and the middle of the block, with the lane outlined (contact sheets made with RESEARCH-only tools, not saved). Street View (Apr and May 2026, Sep 2024, Jul 2022, May 2024 user and car imagery) was used for the streets round the park: Washington Sq N, E and W, LaGuardia Pl, W 4th, W 8th, E 8th and E 9th St show plain white-lined lanes with no green mid-block.
- Results in RESEARCH/roads/bike-paint.json (keyed segment:piece:side): 24 pieces fully green (Lafayette St, Sixth Avenue in part, Washington Sq N and W, Fourth Avenue, Union Sq E, 7th Av S, Broadway at Union Square), 11 green only where cars cross (green skip bars through the intersection and a 6 m green box at the block's start: Sixth Avenue, W 13th St, Bleecker St, First Avenue, E Houston St, Prince St), 93 not green.
- Every lane piece now has New York's white bicycle stencil with an arrow, 5 m into each block in the direction of travel and every 60 m on long blocks (153 stencils), and white flexible delineators every 6 m down painted buffers with no parking beyond them (32 posts). Parked-car protection is the parking lane outside the buffer (parked cars themselves are not modelled yet).
- Problem found: on Union Sq E (pieces 96 to 99 and 103 in RESEARCH/roads/bike-lanes.json) the lane is drawn against the east curb, but the real green lane is the curved one along the west side; left unpainted until lane placement is fixed in the surface sweep.

### Pole audit, second pass (15 Oct 2026)

Avi still saw a pole in the middle of Fifth Avenue at the Arch. The first pass had fixed the sources it knew about; this pass checks the built scene itself.
- How each object gets its position: parking sign posts (NYC DOT points, moved to their own curb), hydrants (NYC DEP, moved when in the road), street lamps (OSM points, moved when in the road), signal poles (corner of each signalised intersection from the centre lines, moved when in the road), bus stop poles (OSM, set inside the curb), street name sign poles (a corner worked out from the centre lines: **the cause of the Fifth Avenue pole and 168 others**, now moved to the curb or dropped), subway stairs (MTA points; a stair whose footprint reaches over the curb slides sideways onto the pavement), benches, baskets and drinking fountains (OSM; moved off the curb line), street trees (NYC Parks forestry points; 372 sat just over the curb line and now stand 0.9 m in, the middle of a tree pit). OSM traffic-signal, stop-sign, crossing and bicycle-parking nodes are not drawn as objects at all.
- dist/campus/curbs.js is the single definition of "roadway" (planimetric roadbed minus sidewalks, plazas, raised medians, parks, gardens and OSM pedestrian areas), shared by the build tools, tests/poles.test.js and the in-game audit.
- tests/poles.test.js (npm test) checks every data source: sign posts, hydrants, bus stop poles, signal poles, street lamps, street name sign poles, benches, baskets, drinking fountains and trees.
- tools/audit-scene.py loads the game headless with ?audit=poles and walks every mesh and instance in the scene (about 83,000 upright objects), failing if anything upright stands in the roadway. Allowed: in-street Citi Bike stations (in the parking lane, where NYC DOT puts them), bike-lane delineators, people and cars, and six surveyed trees where the data has roadbed under real planting (one in the Washington Square Village garden, five on the north edge of Christopher Park).
